Predicting medical insurance charges

An analysis of how demographic and behavioral variables relate to annual U.S. medical insurance charges.

Key finding: Smoking status was the strongest predictor in this model. The estimated difference was about $23,849 in annual charges for smokers, holding the included variables constant.

Question & data

How do age, BMI, children, sex, smoking status, and region relate to annual charges? The team analyzed a public dataset of 1,338 observations.

Approach

Exploratory plots showed distributions and relationships. A multiple linear regression model in R estimated the association of all six predictors with charges, followed by model diagnostics.

0.749

Adjusted R²

The model explained approximately 75% of the variation in charges in this dataset.

Practical interpretation

Smoking was associated with much higher charges; age and BMI also showed positive relationships. The team translated coefficients and visuals into a clear discussion of cost drivers.

Limits of the analysis

The observational, cross-sectional data cannot establish causation. Diagnostics showed nonconstant variance and tail departures from normality, so predictions and coefficient interpretations should be treated with that context.
