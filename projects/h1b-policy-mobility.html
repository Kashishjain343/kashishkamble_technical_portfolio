H-1B policy and Boston-area mobility

A visual exploration of whether foot traffic in Boston and Cambridge tech and university areas changed differently from comparison areas after the September 2025 H-1B policy announcement.

Finding: In the two months compared, median visits changed by less than 1% in both treatment and comparison groups. The analysis did not find a clear localized mobility decline in the selected tech and university zones.

65,360

POI-month observations

The group used Advan SafeGraph mobility data for Boston and Cambridge, comparing January 2025 with January 2026.

Methods

They filtered unreliable low-visit locations, defined treatment and comparison groups, and examined visit changes with maps, distributions, and dwell-time comparisons.

Median visit change

Treatment: +0.48%. Comparison: −0.77%. Both changes were small, with no strong difference in the hypothesized direction.

Visual evidence

The geographic view showed no concentrated cluster of large declines. Treatment and comparison distributions overlapped substantially.

What the result cannot tell us

Only two same-month snapshots were available. The comparison cannot isolate the policy's causal effect or show a continuous trend around the announcement; other influences on mobility may remain.
