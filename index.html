    A little about me

    “Most of what I know about data, I learned by needing it.”

    A stockout I could have seen coming. A supplier who was always late. A factory log nobody could search. Those everyday problems pulled me into analytics, and they're still the problems I like solving most.

    I studied data science and analysis at Jain University in Bengaluru, then went back to the floor: running operations for my family's business in Aurangabad and building tracking tools at Endurance Technologies. Now I'm in the Boston area, finishing my master's at Brandeis.

    Outside class, I'm usually working as an SBE Captain for the business school or on Phonathon calls with alumni. Both keep me practicing the other half of the job: listening first, then explaining clearly.

    How I work

    1. Step 1

      Start with the real question

      Before opening a dataset, I ask what decision it should help someone make. My project titles are questions for that reason.

    2. Step 2

      Be honest about what the data can't say

      In my H-1B study the answer was "no clear effect," and I said so, along with the limits of two snapshots of data.

    3. Step 3

      Make it easy to act on

      A model is only useful if a manager can read it. That's why my optimization ended up in a Power BI dashboard, not a spreadsheet of equations.
